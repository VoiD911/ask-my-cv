Principal Platform Architect, Applied AI (Full-time, Remote within Canada)

About us
Northwind Logistics Cloud builds software that helps mid-sized manufacturers and distributors plan shipments, manage warehouse capacity and keep their customers informed. More than 1,200 companies across North America rely on our platform every day, from family-owned food producers to national building-supply chains. Our product teams are spread across Toronto, Montreal, Halifax and Vancouver, and we have been fully remote-friendly since our founding in 2016.

Over the past eighteen months we have started to embed generative AI into the product. Our first assistant summarizes delivery exceptions for dispatchers; a second one drafts customer notifications when a truck is delayed. Both were built quickly by small teams and proved that customers value the time saved. We now need a principal architect who can turn these experiments into a coherent, secure and cost-aware platform that every product team can build on.

What you will do
Own the reference architecture for AI features across the product, from retrieval pipelines to prompt management, evaluation and observability.
Work with product managers and customer success to identify the business processes where automation brings measurable value, and translate them into technical specifications that teams can deliver incrementally.
Design retrieval-augmented generation services over customer documents such as contracts, shipping manifests and support tickets, with strict tenant isolation.
Define guardrails for every AI feature: input screening against prompt injection, output validation, personal-data protection, rate limiting and spending caps.
Lead the build of an internal agent platform that lets product teams assemble tools, prompts and workflows without reinventing the plumbing each time.
Establish continuous evaluation: regression suites that run on every change, nightly red-team runs, drift monitoring and clear quality gates before release.
Partner with our security and compliance team on threat models, supply-chain security for models and containers, and audit trails.
Mentor senior engineers, run architecture reviews and write decision records that explain the trade-offs behind each choice.
Represent engineering in conversations with enterprise customers who want to understand how their data is handled by our AI features.

A typical week
On Monday you might sit with a dispatcher in one of our customer warehouses to watch how exceptions are really handled, then sketch with the product manager what an assistant could take off their plate. On Tuesday you review a design proposal for a new retrieval index and push back on a shortcut that would mix data from two tenants. Midweek you pair with an engineer to debug why the nightly evaluation suddenly dropped by four points after a model upgrade, and you decide whether to roll back or adjust the prompt. On Thursday you present the quarterly AI cost report to the leadership team, explaining where caching and smaller models saved money and where they did not. Friday is usually reserved for writing: a decision record, a guide for teams adopting the agent platform, or feedback on a pull request that changes how we log model calls.

The first six months
In your first month you will meet every team that ships AI features and map what already exists. By the end of the third month we expect a written target architecture, agreed with engineering leadership, and a roadmap that retires the most fragile parts of our early prototypes. By month six the first product teams should be building on the shared platform, with evaluation and guardrails enabled by default rather than added at the end.

Our stack
Our core services run on AWS, with a growing footprint on Azure for customers who require it. Backend services are written mostly in Python and Go; we use FastAPI for new services, PostgreSQL with pgvector for retrieval, and Redis for caching. Everything ships through GitHub Actions into containers built with Docker. Production workloads run on Amazon EKS, infrastructure is managed with Terraform, and observability is built on OpenTelemetry with traces exported to our tracing backend. We call foundation models through Amazon Bedrock and keep a small set of self-hosted models for latency-sensitive tasks.
